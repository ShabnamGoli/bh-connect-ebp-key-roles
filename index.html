Welcome to Key Stakeholder Roles in EBP Implementation. In this overview, you will learn how behavioral health practitioners, counties, Centers of Excellence, and the Department of Health Care Services work together to support evidence-based practice implementation and service delivery.

Evidence-based practice, or EBP, implementation involves four key stakeholder groups. Each group has distinct responsibilities for supporting implementation, meeting requirements, and delivering services.

Behavioral health practitioners complete required training, meet applicable quality requirements, deliver EBPs, and report required data. Counties coordinate implementation, support practitioners, ensure practitioners meet requirements, and submit Medi-Cal claims and individual and service-level encounters to DHCS. Centers of Excellence develop implementation resources and provide training, technical assistance, and quality support. DHCS establishes requirements and operational guidance, reviews data, oversees compliance, and addresses policy and payment questions.

Together, these four roles form a connected system. DHCS establishes requirements and provides oversight. Centers of Excellence, or COEs, provide training, technical assistance, and quality support. Counties ensure their practitioners meet applicable requirements. Practitioners complete the requirements and deliver EBPs.

Quality requirements differ by EBP

Objective 1: Identify the applicable quality-review process for each EBP.

Different EBPs use different processes to confirm that required standards are met.

Fidelity monitoring

Checks whether services follow the evidence-based model.

Applies to

ACT, FACT, CSC, IPS, and HFW

Accreditation or certification

Confirms that formal standards have been met.

Applies to

  • Accreditation: Clubhouse Services
  • Certification: MST, FFT, and PCIT

Training
DHCS
ACT, FACT, CSC, IPS, and HFW: establish training requirements, review COE training data, oversee COE training delivery, and oversee county compliance. Clubhouse Services, MST, FFT, and PCIT: review COE training data and oversee COE training delivery.
COEs
Develop and deliver required training; track completion; send reports.
Counties
Ensure practitioners complete training; review reports.
Practitioners
Complete required training.

Training responsibilities differ by EBP group. Review the complete DHCS training roles and requirements.

Quality oversight

Fidelity monitoring

DHCS
Set requirements; review fidelity data from COEs; oversee compliance.
COEs
Develop and conduct assessments; coordinate scheduling; track status; send reports.
Counties
Ensure assessments occur on required timelines; review reports.
Practitioners
Complete assessments on schedule; review reports.

Accreditation or certification

DHCS
Set requirements; review COE data; oversee compliance.
COEs
Develop and conduct the process; track status; send reports.
Counties
Ensure practitioners complete the required process; review reports.
Practitioners
Complete required processes; review reports.
Service Delivery
DHCS
Set requirements and operational guidance; oversee compliance.
COEs
Support practitioners in delivering services and support counties in establishing EBP teams, HFW provider sites, or EBP programs, as applicable.
Counties
Ensure practitioners deliver services consistent with DHCS requirements.
Practitioners
Identify and enroll individuals for whom EBPs are medically necessary; deliver EBPs consistent with standards and DHCS requirements.
