Welcome to Key Stakeholder Roles in EBP Implementation. In the next four minutes, you will learn how behavioral health practitioners, counties, Centers of Excellence, and DHCS contribute to implementing evidence-based practices—and how their responsibilities differ.

Evidence-based practice, or EBP, implementation involves four key stakeholder groups. Each group has distinct responsibilities for supporting implementation, meeting requirements, and delivering services.

Behavioral health practitioners complete required training, meet applicable quality requirements, deliver EBPs, and report required data. Counties coordinate implementation, ensure practitioners meet requirements, and submit Medi-Cal claims and individual- and service-level encounters to DHCS. Centers of Excellence develop implementation resources and provide training, technical assistance, and quality support. DHCS establishes requirements and operational guidance, reviews data, oversees compliance, and addresses policy and payment questions.

Together, these four roles form a connected system. DHCS establishes requirements and provides oversight. Centers of Excellence, or COEs, provide training, technical assistance, and quality support. Counties ensure their practitioners meet applicable requirements. Practitioners complete the requirements and deliver EBPs.

Now that you have seen how the four stakeholder roles connect, continue to the interactive activity to examine each stakeholder’s responsibilities in greater detail. Select each role to learn how practitioners, counties, Centers of Excellence, and DHCS contribute to EBP implementation.

The core relationship, in one line

The four groups aren't independent — they form a chain. Every process in the source guidance, from training to fidelity monitoring to payment, follows this same line of responsibility:

  1. DHCS sets requirements
  2. →
  3. COEs deliver training, TA & fidelity support
  4. →
  5. Counties ensure compliance
  6. →
  7. Practitioners complete requirements & deliver care

The four roles

Objective 1: by the end of this section, you'll be able to identify each role and its primary function.

Behavioral Health Practitioners

Contract with counties to deliver specified EBPs. Complete required training and fidelity assessments. Deliver services consistent with evidence-based standards.

Counties

Contract with practitioners to deliver EBPs. Ensure practitioners complete training, fidelity, and data requirements. Report to DHCS.

Centers of Excellence (COEs)

Develop and deliver required training and technical assistance. Conduct fidelity assessments. Track status and send reports to practitioners, counties, and DHCS.

DHCS

Establishes EBP requirements and operational guidance. Oversees COEs and county compliance statewide. Answers policy and payment questions.

See it in action

Objective 2: expand each process area below and compare what each role owns. Notice how the same DHCS → COE → County → Practitioner chain repeats every time.

Training
Practitioners
Complete required trainings (foundational, role-based, ongoing).
Counties
Ensure practitioners complete required trainings; review training reports.
COEs
Develop and deliver required trainings; track completion; send reports.
DHCS
Sets training requirements; oversees COEs and county compliance.
Fidelity Monitoring
Practitioners
Complete required fidelity assessments on schedule; review fidelity reports.
Counties
Ensure practitioners meet fidelity timelines; review fidelity reports.
COEs
Conduct fidelity assessments; coordinate scheduling; send reports.
DHCS
Sets fidelity requirements; reviews fidelity data statewide.
Service Delivery
Practitioners
Identify and enroll eligible individuals; deliver EBPs to standard.
Counties
Ensure practitioners deliver services consistent with DHCS requirements.
COEs
Support practitioners and counties in establishing quality service delivery.
DHCS
Sets operational guidance; oversees statewide compliance.
